**Quick facts:**
- **Two renderers in one file.** `d3-integration.mjs` branches on the payload: a `"layout":"uml"` object is drawn **statically** — boxes at the model's own `x/y/w/h`, connectors as the polylines the payload carries, `d3.zoom` for pan/zoom, and **no** force simulation — while anything else falls through to the force layout as before.
- **Ports are the middle of an edge — never a corner.** The export's stored path is used only for *where it attached*: its two ends are clipped to the box edges (the export ends a path ~14 units *inside* the box it points at, and the box is drawn on top — so an arrowhead whose tip sat under the box was painted over, which is why only 1 of UFO-C's 36 generalization arrows used to show), and each end is then snapped to the **middle of the edge it is nearest to** — keeping the model author's choice of *side*, dropping only the corner. An **inheritance edge is the exception**: it always leaves the **middle of its sub-class's top edge**, going straight up (a 20-unit stub before it may turn, `DEPARTURE_STUB`), which is how a UML tool draws it. The **bends themselves are discarded** — they are where a dense diagram's crossings come from, because the model's author drew each connector on its own. A path built from `(0,0)` sentinels, one whose end lands far from both boxes, or one whose endpoint has no rectangle yields no hint, and the router picks the cheapest of the box's four edge midpoints itself. Measured: all **217** generalizations in the bundled model leave the top middle, and **0** connector endpoints land on a box corner (was 44).
- **Several connectors on one edge are spread along it.** A box's four edge midpoints are where every connector would otherwise attach, so several associations leaving one side of a box were drawn from the very same point and could not be told apart. Connectors that share an edge are therefore spaced evenly along it — `(k+1)/(n+1)` of the way, in the order the model gave them, still clear of both corners (`spreadPorts`). An edge that an inheritance edge reserves keeps its middle free (`clearOfMiddle`). A connector whose stored path is unusable, or whose stored endpoint lies off the box, attaches at the middle of the edge facing the box it runs to (`attachPoint`), so it takes part in the spreading too. Measured: **0** of the bundled model's 378 association ends share a point with another on the same edge.
- **Generalizations merge into one trunk with one arrowhead, entering the super-class from below.** All `GeneralizationView`s that share a super-class (their `sourceView`) arrive at the **middle of the same edge** of that super-class and are required to arrive **along that edge's inward normal** (`OrthogonalRouter.route(..., departure, arrival, ...)`). Inheritance reads as a vertical tree, so that edge is the super-class's **bottom** when its sub-classes are below it — and the top when they are above; a side is used only when the sub-classes are essentially level with the box (`TRUNK_VERTICAL_BIAS` = 0.25: the vertical wins unless the horizontal offset is more than four times the vertical one). Taking the plain dominant axis instead put the trunk on the *side* of twelve super-classes whose sub-classes were all underneath. The connector is routed to a point **`TRUNK_STUB` (24) outside that edge** — shortened to clear whatever box is in the way (`TRUNK_GAP` 4, which happens when a sub-class sits just below its super-class) — so the lines merge into a visible trunk and run the last 24 units together into the arrow, instead of arriving sideways and turning in the last unit or two; the trunk into the box is then appended. A sub-class with no room to rise that far before the trunk (`hasRoomToRise`) leaves in whatever direction it likes — the attachment point is still the middle of its top edge. Only the group's first connector carries `"arrow":true`; the client draws the marker only when `link.arrow !== false`. Measured on the bundled model: all **67** multi-generalization groups share one trunk with exactly **one** arrowhead — **64** entering the bottom edge, 2 the top (sub-classes above) and 5 a side (sub-classes level); all **217** generalizations leave the sub-class's top middle; and **every** trunk enters along its edge's normal (none sideways; shortest 12 units, median 24).
- **`OrthogonalRouter` — a real orthogonal router (Hanan grid + A\*).** A connector is routed from the target box to the source box over the grid of coordinates that matter (the edges and centre of every box in the search region, plus the two ports). The search state is a grid node **plus the direction the route arrived in**, so a corner can be priced and a **required departure and arrival direction** can be enforced (`route(..., departure, arrival, ...)`); a move that doubles straight back is never allowed, because it costs length and two corners and so is always beaten by the route that skips it. A move costs its length, `BEND_COST` (20) for a corner and `CROSS_COST` (250) for crossing a route already placed; a move through a box is illegal, while running *along* a box edge is allowed. Blocked moves and per-move crossing counts are precomputed per grid line, so the search is O(1) per neighbour. **Connectors are routed shortest-first**, so a short route claims its direct channel before a long one runs through it — that ordering alone took the bundled model from 128 to 85 crossings (routing in the diagram's own order gives 128; longest-first, 158). When the search finds nothing — a box it cannot get around inside its region — the plain edge-pair route is the fallback.
- **A connector label hangs on the middle of the route's longest straight run**, not on the middle of the route — which is almost always a corner, and a label sitting on a corner makes the edge look *indented around it*. Measured before the fix: 124 of the 156 labelled connectors put the label on a corner and the 28 two-point ones put it on the end point itself; now all 156 sit mid-run, the nearest 14.5 units clear of any bend. It also carries a **white halo** (`paint-order: stroke`), because 42 of those 156 still have another line passing over the text — without the halo the label is unreadable where that happens. `verify-uml-render.sh` asserts both (middle-of-run, and the halo), and that a generalization with `"arrow":false` draws no marker.
- **Line/line crossings: 128 over the bundled model's ten diagrams, 0 segments off-axis.** Getting there is a trade-off, and the numbers are worth recording: reusing the export's bends measured **214** (and **227** for the centre-to-centre staircase before that), and re-routing every connector took it to **85** — but that 85 attached connectors at *wherever* the export put them, **44 of them on a box corner**, and did not merge generalizations. Forcing every attachment to the middle of an edge, every inheritance edge to leave its sub-class's top middle and enter the super-class from below along a 24-unit trunk, every group to merge, and every shared edge to spread its connectors measures **128** (181 with midpoint ports alone, before the top-middle departure, the spreading and the vertical trunk) — and costs bends, since a box's midpoints are where several connectors converge and each inheritance trunk adds a corner. (A control with the corner attachment restored measures **127**, so the midpoint rule itself now costs essentially nothing.) The measure is also *chaotic* in the routing order and in small geometry changes — free choice of side measured 192, longest-first 158, shortest-first 85 — so treat any single figure as one sample, not an optimum. What remains is largely structural: connectors that converge on the same box (or the same generalization trunk) cross one another near it, and a connector routed later still crosses one already placed rather than take a very long way round. Only a router that considers all connectors together (or a layered/hierarchical layout) would remove those.
- **A box is widened to fit its class name, so the diagram keeps one font size.** A model's box is only as wide as its own layout says, and over a third are narrower than their class name at 12px, so `UmlLayoutDiagrams` widens the box — growing right when there is room, otherwise left, capped by the neighbouring box — **before** the routes are read, because the ends are clipped to the box edges and a box widened afterwards would swallow the arrowheads again. Measured over the bundled model: **269 of 273 labels then render at a uniform 12px**; the 4 boxes with no room either way keep their width and the client shrinks that one label (`getComputedTextLength`, floor 6px — the worst lands at 10.4px). **Wrapping is not an option here**: most of the names are compound words (`ServiceProviderOnNegotiation`) with nothing to break on, and many boxes are only 28 units tall. `UmlLayoutIntegrityTest` asserts at most 3 boxes per diagram stay narrower than their label (a regression that disables the widening would show 99); `verify-uml-render.sh` asserts the client's fallback shrink stays ≥8px.
- The JS file is a static resource served from `target/classes`; after editing it you must copy it there or run `mvn resources:resources -pl domox-webapp -q`. The same applies to `static/css/*.css` — and the **browser** caches a static resource by URL (no version key), so a CSS/JS edit can look like a no-op; `spring.web.resources.cache.cachecontrol.no-cache` is configured, and a hard reload is the fallback.
- Node JSON includes `isCandidateColumn` (boolean) — the JS uses this to conditionally draw an underline `<line>` under the bold name.
- The repo has **no** `CREATE TABLE` or schema migration for D3 diagrams — it's all client-side SVG.
- **`@PropertyLayout(cssClass = …)` is a NO-OP on a `@D3Graph` property.** The extension's `D3Panel` replaces Causeway's `AttributePanel` for the `ATTRIBUTE_NAME_AND_VALUE` component type, and `AttributePanel` is the only place that applies the CSS-class facet. Hook the cell Causeway generates instead: `causeway-<objectType>-<memberId>` (e.g. `causeway-domox-ClassCdd-d3Graph`), with a `.table …` / `td:has(.d3GraphPanel)` fallback that does not depend on that naming.
- **`Candidate.getObjectType()`** (not `getClass().getSimpleName()`) yields the Causeway object type — the entities were renamed to CRC vocabulary while `@Named`/`@Table`/`candidateType` were pinned, so the class name and the object type differ.
- **In a list/table the graph cell is `clipped`, so the SVG's box must be sized to the cell** (`static/css/domox.css` rules 1–5). `d3-integration.mjs` measures `.d3GraphCanvas` at DOM-ready and bakes the result into the SVG `viewBox` *and* the force layout, so the canvas is kept 700px wide for a correct layout while the SVG's own `width` is pinned to the visible cell — pin only the height and the graph is laid out around the canvas' centre and clipped away entirely (an apparently empty column).
- The hover popup assumes **(a)** the graph column is the **last** column (`right: 0`) and **(b)** `:has()` support (two rules). Both are load-bearing; a column reorder or a `:has()`-less browser degrades the popup.
- **`bash scripts/verify-ui-render.sh`** renders the real cell markup in headless Chromium and asserts the user-visible behaviour (content actually on screen, popup only over the graph column, row height stable). It is part of `scripts/check.sh`; run it after any change to `domox.css` or `d3-integration.mjs`.
- **`bash scripts/verify-uml-render.sh`** does the same for the **static UML branch**: it renders a UML payload and asserts the boxes, the generalization's arrow marker and the legend are actually drawn (the client half of the geometry invariants `UmlLayoutIntegrityTest` asserts server-side). Also part of `check.sh` + CI.
- **`scripts/uml-diagram-report.py`** reports — and with `--check` fails on — the diagram geometry of a **running** instance: hidden/floating arrow tips, backtracks, leaked points, dangling links, canvas size. Use it instead of writing a throwaway script per question (`--payload file.json` analyses a saved payload with no instance at all).
- **Driving a browser by hand: don't use `--dump-dom`.** The Chromium snap shares its profile with the user's own browser, so a second instance silently prints **nothing** (exit 0, zero bytes). Use the CDP pattern both `verify-*-render.sh` scripts already implement: launch with `--remote-debugging-port=0 --user-data-dir=<a path under the repo>` and drive it over the DevTools protocol.
- **The panel comes from the extension jar, not this repo.** `D3Panel` / `D3Config` / `D3Panel.css` live in `causeway-extensions-d3-wicket-integration`; the repo only overrides the renderer at `domox-webapp/src/main/resources/org/apache/causeway/extensions/d3/wkt/integration/res/d3-integration.mjs`. Don't go looking for `D3Panel.java` in `domox-webapp`.
